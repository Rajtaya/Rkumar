Research

Research

My research sits in theoretical computer science — how computation is modelled, how hard problems are, and how systems can be formally verified — with applied work in search-based software engineering. Below are my primary areas, additional interests, and current directions.

Primary areas

Theoretical computer science

Automata Theory

Formal models of computation and the languages they recognise — with particular attention to pushdown automata and their extensions to multiple stacks.

  • Pushdown & multi-pushdown systems
  • Formal languages
  • LEX / YACC parsing
  • Computational models

Related work: “One-Way Tape Deterministic PDA” (2023), “Automatic Scanning and Parsing using LEX and YACC” (2017), “State Complexity of Unary and Binary Operations on Regular Languages” (2015).

Computational Complexity

Measuring the resources computation requires — the complexity of Turing machines and of operations on formal languages and grammars.

  • Turing-machine complexity
  • Complexity of language operations
  • Algorithmic complexity

Related work: “Complexity of Finite State Turing Machine with Other Domain” (2026), “Complexity of Σ*Ā and its Connection with Logic” (2017), “Complexity Measure of Turing Machine” (2015).

Formal Verification

Proving properties of concurrent and infinite-state systems through reachability analysis under bounded restrictions that keep verification decidable.

  • Reachability analysis
  • Context-bounded analysis
  • Round-bounded analysis
  • Scope-bounded analysis

An emerging direction — see Current research directions below.

Current research directions ongoing — not yet published

Multi-pushdown systems & bounded reachability

My current work extends classical pushdown automata to multi-pushdown systems, which model concurrent programs with several recursive threads. Because reachability is undecidable for such systems in general, the research studies bounded restrictions — context-bounded, round-bounded, and scope-bounded reachability — that recover decidability while remaining expressive enough to capture realistic behaviour.

This connects my long-standing interest in automata and complexity with formal verification. These are active, unpublished directions; results will be listed under Publications as they appear.
